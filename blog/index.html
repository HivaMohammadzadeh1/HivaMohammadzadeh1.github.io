A ten-part series, written from first principles

Deep Learning from the Inside Out

I take modern language models apart — the transformer, BERT, GPT-2, T5, and LLaMA — down to their weight matrices, tensor shapes, and FLOP counts. Every claim here is one you can check with arithmetic.

Written from the presentations and the CS199 independent study I did at UC Berkeley EECS in 2023, surveying eleven papers from Attention Is All You Need through GPT-4.

Hiva Mohammadzadeh  ·  Stanford MSCS, UC Berkeley EECS  ·  ten deep dives, four architectures

Contents

Foundations

00
The Transformer: where attention replaced recurrence

Self-attention and multi-head attention built from first principles, and the three model families every modern LLM descends from. Attention Is All You Need, encoder vs decoder.

01
A survey of activation functions

The nonlinearities that let a network learn curves, from sigmoid and tanh through ReLU, GeLU, and Swish, with the plots and the trade-offs. Derivatives and why smoothness matters.

02
Normalization, from BatchNorm to RMSNorm

Why activations drift during training, and how each normalizer fixes it. BatchNorm, LayerNorm, GroupNorm, RMSNorm.

07
GLUE and SuperGLUE: measuring language understanding

The benchmarks that defined the field, task by task, with real examples, dataset sizes, and the metric each one is scored on. Nine GLUE tasks, eight SuperGLUE tasks.

Model architectures

03
BERT: an encoder that reads both directions

The full data path from input embeddings through self-attention and feed-forward layers, with exact tensor shapes and a FLOP count for every block. 12 blocks, 768-dim, 1.86B FLOPs per block.

04
GPT-2: a decoder that writes one token at a time

The causal mask, the full computation diagram, and how a decoder-only model's FLOPs compare against BERT's encoder. Autoregressive generation, 1.51B FLOPs per block.

05
T5: one encoder, one decoder, joined by cross-attention

Encoder self-attention, decoder masked attention, and the cross-attention bridge, plus span corruption and prefix conditioning. Text-to-text.

06
LLaMA: smaller models, more data, three sharp ideas

RMSNorm, SwiGLU, and rotary position embeddings, traced through the forward pass. The recipe that showed data beats raw parameter count. 7B to 65B parameters.

Techniques

08
Prompt tuning: from fine-tuning to chain-of-thought

Full fine-tuning, soft prompts, prefix tuning, and chain-of-thought, ending with the MedPrompt result that pushed GPT-4 to 90.2% on medical QA. Parameter-efficient prompting.

09
Scaling: from GPT-3 to GPT-4

What changes when a decoder grows from millions to hundreds of billions of parameters: few-shot learning, multimodality, and the risks that come with scale. GPT-3, PaLM, GPT-4.
